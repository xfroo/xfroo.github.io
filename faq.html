Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about playing Meteor Swerve, saving your progress and racing online. Tap a question to open its answer.

The game

What is Meteor Swerve?

Meteor Swerve is a free arcade game in which you steer a small ship through falling meteor storms. It has a 48-sector campaign across six worlds with a boss in each, an endless mode, online races for up to four players, a daily challenge and a hangar full of ships and upgrades. It runs in your web browser. See how to play for the basics.

Is it free? Are there ads?

Yes, the whole game is free to play, and everything in it is earned by playing: there is nothing to buy with real money. Ads help pay for the game's development. Any ads appear only between sectors or when you choose to watch one for a reward, never while you are flying.

Who makes Meteor Swerve?

Meteor Swerve is made by xfroo, an independent developer, and it is updated regularly. You can read about the game's history on the updates page and more about the project on the about page.

Is Meteor Swerve suitable for children?

The game is made for a general audience. There is no blood or realistic violence, just rocks bursting into sparks. There is no chat and no way to send messages to other players, pilot names are made up by the game, and the game never asks for a name, email address or any other personal details.

Devices and settings

Which devices and browsers does it work on?

Meteor Swerve works on phones, tablets and computers in any modern browser, including Safari on iPhone and iPad, Chrome on Android, and Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on computers. It adapts to the screen: on a wide screen, panels beside the play area show your progress, coins and power-ups. It runs just as smoothly on 60 Hz and 120 Hz screens.

Can I install it like an app?

Yes. Installing it puts an icon on your home screen and opens the game full screen, without the browser's address bar.

  • iPhone and iPad: open the game in Safari, tap the Share button and choose Add to Home Screen.
  • Android: open the game in Chrome, open the menu (⋮) and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
  • Computer: Chrome and Edge can install it too, from the install option in the browser's menu.

On iPhone and iPad the home-screen app keeps its own save, separate from Safari's. Use a backup code (see below) to move your progress between them.

Does it work offline?

You need an internet connection to open the game. Once it has loaded, single-player modes keep working even if your connection drops, and so do races against the bots. Online races need a connection the whole time.

How do I change the language?

The game speaks English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German and Hebrew. It picks your browser's language the first time you play. To change it, press the language name at the very bottom of the main menu and choose another one.

How do I turn off the sound or the music?

The main menu and the pause screen both have a speaker button for sound effects and a music-note button for the music. They are separate, so you can keep the sound effects and turn off the music, or the other way round.

The game feels slow or choppy. What can I do?

Meteor Swerve is light, but older phones can struggle when lots of other things are running. Close other browser tabs and apps, turn off battery saver or Low Power Mode while you play, and make sure your browser is up to date.

Progress

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser's storage on your own device. There is no account and no server, so nothing about your game leaves your device. The flip side is that clearing your browser's data for the site, or playing in a private window, starts you from scratch.

How do I move my progress to another phone or computer?

Tap the save icon at the bottom of the main menu. Press Copy my code to copy your backup code and send it to yourself or keep it in your notes. On the other device, open the same screen, paste the code into the second box and press Restore this code. Coins, stars, ships, trails, upgrades and skills all come across.

I lost my progress. Can you get it back?

Unfortunately not. Progress is only ever stored on your device, so there is no copy anywhere else. If you saved a backup code earlier, you can restore it from the save screen. To avoid losing progress in future, copy a fresh backup code now and then.

Is there a leaderboard?

There is no global leaderboard. The daily challenge is the place to compare yourself with others: everyone flies the same storm each day, and the Share button sends your time with a link so friends can try to beat it on the very same storm.

Online play

How do I play with my friends?

Press Online race, then Create room. Send your friends the four-letter room code or press Copy invite link and send the link. When they have joined, press Start race. Up to four pilots can race at once. The online races guide has all the details.

Why can't I connect to a room?

The most common reasons are a mistyped code, a room whose host has left, or a network that blocks direct connections between devices, which is common on school and office networks. Try switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data. The connection section of the races guide explains each error message.

Is online play fair?

Yes. In a race every ship has the same three hull cells, and ship perks, upgrades and skills are switched off, so a new player and a veteran fly with identical ships. The game also drops players whose game sends impossible moves.

This website

The game used to open at xfroo.github.io. Where did it go?

Since version 3.5 (27 November 2026) the game itself lives at xfroo.github.io/play.html, and the main address is this website with guides. Old invite links, daily challenge links and home-screen apps still take you straight to the game, and your progress is untouched.

How do I report a bug or suggest an idea?

Open an issue on the game's GitHub page at github.com/xfroo/xfroo.github.io/issues (a free GitHub account is needed). Say which device and browser you use and what happened, and include a screenshot if you can.

What data does the game collect?

Very little. The site counts visits without cookies, and online races connect players' devices to each other. The privacy policy explains everything, including how ads use cookies.

The best way to find out is to play a few sectors.
